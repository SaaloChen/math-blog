Sobolev Spaces — Week 2, Problem 1

Sobolev Spaces

Week 2
Weak convergence together with convergence of norms and its relation to strong convergence in Banach and L^p spaces.
Published

September 25, 2026

Problem

Let \(X\) be a Banach space and suppose that

\[ u_k\rightharpoonup u \quad\text{weakly in }X \]

and

\[ \|u_k\|_X\to \|u\|_X. \]

Does it follow that

\[ u_k\to u \quad\text{strongly in }X? \]

In particular, what happens when \(X=L^p(\Omega)\) for \(1\leq p\leq\infty\)?

Solution

In a general Banach space, the conclusion is false.

For \(L^p(\Omega)\), the situation is as follows:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} 1<p<\infty &: \quad \text{the implication is true},\\ p=1 &: \quad \text{the implication is false in general},\\ p=\infty &: \quad \text{the implication is false in general}. \end{aligned}} \]

The positive result for \(1<p<\infty\) follows from the uniform convexity of \(L^p\).

The case \(1<p<\infty\)

For every \(1<p<\infty\), the space \(L^p(\Omega)\) is uniformly convex. We prove more generally that in every uniformly convex Banach space,

\[ u_k\rightharpoonup u, \qquad \|u_k\|_X\to\|u\|_X \]

imply

\[ u_k\to u \quad\text{strongly in }X. \]

If \(u=0\), then

\[ \|u_k-u\|_X=\|u_k\|_X\to0, \]

so the conclusion is immediate.

Suppose now that \(\|u\|_X>0\). Define

\[ v_k=\frac{u_k}{\|u_k\|_X}, \qquad v=\frac{u}{\|u\|_X}. \]

Since

\[ \|u_k\|_X\to\|u\|_X>0, \]

we have

\[ v_k\rightharpoonup v, \]

and

\[ \|v_k\|_X=\|v\|_X=1. \]

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(v_k\) does not converge strongly to \(v\). Passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists \(\varepsilon>0\) such that

\[ \|v_k-v\|_X\geq\varepsilon \]

for every \(k\).

By uniform convexity, there exists \(\delta>0\) such that

\[ \left\| \frac{v_k+v}{2} \right\|_X \leq 1-\delta. \]

On the other hand,

\[ \frac{v_k+v}{2}\rightharpoonup v. \]

Since the norm is weakly lower semicontinuous,

\[ \|v\|_X \leq \liminf_{k\to\infty} \left\| \frac{v_k+v}{2} \right\|_X. \]

Therefore,

\[ 1 \leq \liminf_{k\to\infty} \left\| \frac{v_k+v}{2} \right\|_X \leq 1-\delta, \]

which is impossible.

Hence

\[ v_k\to v \quad\text{strongly in }X. \]

Finally,

\[ \begin{aligned} \|u_k-u\|_X &= \left\| \|u_k\|_Xv_k-\|u\|_Xv \right\|_X\\ &\leq \|u_k\|_X\|v_k-v\|_X + \left| \|u_k\|_X-\|u\|_X \right|. \end{aligned} \]

Both terms tend to zero. Thus

\[ u_k\to u \quad\text{strongly in }X. \]

Since \(L^p(\Omega)\) is uniformly convex whenever \(1<p<\infty\), we obtain

\[ \boxed{ u_k\rightharpoonup u \text{ in }L^p, \quad \|u_k\|_{L^p}\to\|u\|_{L^p} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_k\to u \text{ in }L^p } \]

for every \(1<p<\infty\).

The case \(p=1\)

The conclusion fails in \(L^1\).

Let \(r_k\) be the \(k\)-th Rademacher function on \((0,1)\). Thus \(r_k\) takes only the values \(1\) and \(-1\), with increasingly rapid dyadic oscillations.

We first observe that

\[ r_k\rightharpoonup0 \quad\text{weakly in }L^1(0,1). \]

Indeed, let \(\varphi\in L^\infty(0,1)\). Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose a dyadic step function \(h\) such that

\[ \|\varphi-h\|_{L^1}<\varepsilon. \]

For sufficiently large \(k\), the function \(r_k\) has zero average on each dyadic interval on which \(h\) is constant. Hence

\[ \int_0^1 h r_k\,dx=0. \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} \left| \int_0^1\varphi r_k\,dx \right| &= \left| \int_0^1(\varphi-h)r_k\,dx \right|\\ &\leq \|\varphi-h\|_{L^1} <\varepsilon. \end{aligned} \]

Thus

\[ r_k\rightharpoonup0 \quad\text{in }L^1(0,1). \]

Now define

\[ u_k=1+r_k, \qquad u=1. \]

Then

\[ u_k\rightharpoonup u \quad\text{in }L^1(0,1). \]

Moreover, \(u_k\) takes the values \(0\) and \(2\), each on a set of measure \(1/2\), so

\[ \|u_k\|_{L^1}=1=\|u\|_{L^1}. \]

However,

\[ \|u_k-u\|_{L^1} = \|r_k\|_{L^1} = 1 \]

for every \(k\).

Hence

\[ u_k\not\to u \quad\text{strongly in }L^1(0,1). \]

Therefore the implication fails for \(p=1\).

The case \(p=\infty\)

The conclusion also fails for \(p=\infty\).

Consider

\[ \ell^\infty = L^\infty(\mathbb N), \]

where \(\mathbb N\) is equipped with counting measure. Let \(e_k\) denote the \(k\)-th standard basis vector,

\[ e_k=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots). \]

We first claim that

\[ e_k\rightharpoonup0 \quad\text{weakly in }\ell^\infty. \]

Let

\[ \Phi\in(\ell^\infty)^*, \]

and set

\[ a_k=\Phi(e_k). \]

For every finite set \(F\subset\mathbb N\), choose scalars \(\varepsilon_k\) with

\[ |\varepsilon_k|=1 \]

such that

\[ \varepsilon_k a_k=|a_k|. \]

Then

\[ \left\| \sum_{k\in F}\varepsilon_k e_k \right\|_{\ell^\infty} =1. \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k\in F}|a_k| &= \Phi\left( \sum_{k\in F}\varepsilon_k e_k \right)\\ &\leq \|\Phi\|. \end{aligned} \]

It follows that

\[ \sum_{k=1}^\infty|\Phi(e_k)|<\infty. \]

In particular,

\[ \Phi(e_k)\to0. \]

Since this holds for every \(\Phi\in(\ell^\infty)^*\),

\[ e_k\rightharpoonup0 \quad\text{in }\ell^\infty. \]

Now let

\[ u=(1,1,1,\ldots) \]

and define

\[ u_k=u-2e_k. \]

Since \(e_k\rightharpoonup0\),

\[ u_k\rightharpoonup u \quad\text{weakly in }\ell^\infty. \]

Every coordinate of \(u_k\) is equal to \(1\), except for the \(k\)-th coordinate, which is \(-1\). Therefore,

\[ \|u_k\|_{\ell^\infty} = 1 = \|u\|_{\ell^\infty}. \]

Thus the norms converge. However,

\[ \|u_k-u\|_{\ell^\infty} = \|-2e_k\|_{\ell^\infty} = 2 \]

for every \(k\).

Consequently,

\[ u_k\not\to u \quad\text{strongly in }\ell^\infty. \]

Hence the implication fails for \(p=\infty\).

Remark

The property

\[ u_k\rightharpoonup u, \qquad \|u_k\|\to\|u\| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_k\to u \]

is known as the Radon–Riesz property, or the Kadec–Klee property.

Every uniformly convex Banach space has this property. In particular,

\[ \boxed{ L^p(\Omega)\text{ has this property for }1<p<\infty. } \]

In contrast, \(L^1\) and \(L^\infty\) do not have this property in general.

The Hilbert space case \(p=2\) is especially simple. Indeed,

\[ \|u_k-u\|_{L^2}^2 = \|u_k\|_{L^2}^2 + \|u\|_{L^2}^2 - 2\langle u_k,u\rangle. \]

Weak convergence gives

\[ \langle u_k,u\rangle \to \|u\|_{L^2}^2, \]

while convergence of the norms gives

\[ \|u_k\|_{L^2}^2 \to \|u\|_{L^2}^2. \]

Hence

\[ \|u_k-u\|_{L^2}\to0. \]

One should also distinguish weak convergence from weak-* convergence in \(L^\infty\). Weak-* convergence is tested against the chosen predual, whereas weak convergence is tested against the entire dual \((L^\infty)^*\). The counterexample above uses genuine weak convergence in \(\ell^\infty\).
